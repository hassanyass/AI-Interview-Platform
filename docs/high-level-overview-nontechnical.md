# Himma — How It Works (non-technical)

Audience: HR leadership, candidates, investors — anyone who should understand the *value flow*, not the implementation. No technology names, versions, protocols, or acronyms belong on this one — if a word wouldn't appear in a product pitch, it doesn't belong here (that's the entire difference from `data-flow-diagram.md`/`architecture-overview.md`, which are the engineering version of this same story).

---

## The story in one line

A company posts a role once, and every candidate gets the same live, AI-conducted voice interview — scored consistently, reviewed by a human before any decision is made.

---

## The four stages (this is the whole diagram)

1. **Post the role** — HR describes the job once; the platform prepares a tailored interview for it.
2. **Candidate applies** — Either applies directly or receives a personal invitation. They open a link, nothing to install.
3. **Live AI interview** — The candidate has a real spoken conversation with an AI interviewer, at a time that suits them. No recruiter needs to be present.
4. **Scored review, human decision** — HR sees a clear scorecard and the full conversation, and decides who moves forward. The AI informs the decision; it never makes it alone.

## What each side actually gets (the value, not the mechanics)

| | HR admin | Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Set up the role and what "good" looks like, once | Apply/receive an invite, on their own time |
| During | Nothing to schedule or attend | A real conversation, not a form |
| After | A consistent, comparable scorecard per candidate | A fair shot — same questions, same standard, as everyone else |

## Tone/visual direction for this one

- Warm, human, editorial — this is a hiring story, not a systems diagram. Photography or simple figurative illustration (people, a conversation bubble, a checklist) fits better than boxes-and-arrows.
- Four stages, left to right or as a simple upward arc — a journey, not a flowchart.
- One sentence of benefit under each stage, not a feature list.
- No box should ever need a legend to be understood.
